        Not a ladder: a braided river

          What's in your DNA?

          Pick where your ancestors come from. These are group figures from published studies, not a genetic test: individuals vary, and mixed ancestry mixes the figures too.

          Breathing thin air

          Many Tibetans carry a version of the EPAS1 gene inherited from Denisovans. It helps the body cope with low oxygen at high altitude, fitting for a people who lived 3,280 m up on the Tibetan Plateau for about 160,000 years.

          Immunity, skin and metabolism

          The Neanderthal variants that became common in people today are mostly linked to immune function, skin and metabolism. Neanderthals had lived in Eurasia, with its germs, sunlight and cold, for hundreds of thousands of years before Homo sapiens arrived.

          What got weeded out

          Right after mixing, early Homo sapiens outside Africa carried more Neanderthal DNA. Much of it was harmful in a sapiens body and was removed by natural selection over generations, leaving the 1 to 2% seen today.

          How each one ended

          "Last seen" is the youngest reliable evidence, not a death date. For most species the real cause is unknown, and several are best described as absorbed rather than wiped out.

          How we date belief

          Radiocarbon

          Living things take in a little radioactive carbon-14, which decays at a known rate after death. Measuring what is left dates when the organism died: a parchment's date is the date of the animal whose skin was used, not of the writing, which came later.

          Dated inscriptions

          Some texts carry their own date, such as a king's year of reign or, at Philae, a calendar date in the demotic text. Others can be dated by what they record: the last cuneiform almanac is dated by the astronomical events it predicts.

          Paleography

          Handwriting and letter shapes change over time. Comparing a text's script with securely dated examples places it within a range, usually decades wide, as with Papyrus P52 or the Ketef Hinnom scrolls.

          Luminescence and ESR

          For the oldest burials, these methods measure the electric charge that natural radiation slowly traps in burnt flint or tooth enamel. They date the layer, with margins of thousands of years.

          Why grade B has error bars

          Handwriting, language and reconstructions from later texts give a plausible range, not a year, so the chart draws them with error bars. Grade A dates come from radiocarbon, dated inscriptions or securely dated archaeology. Traditional dates (grade C) are never plotted.

          On uncertainty

          Ranges and dates are simplified for the map. Species boundaries in fossils are blurry and many dates carry wide error bars. "Last seen" means the youngest evidence found so far. Names are debated too: Homo heidelbergensis may not be a single species (African fossils are sometimes called Homo rhodesiensis), and Homo longi has been proposed as the formal name for Denisovans. Claims marked contested include Homo naledi burials and engravings, Neanderthal authorship of the Maltravieso hand stencil, the Apidima skull from Greece as Homo sapiens, and Homo heidelbergensis as one species. Brain sizes and heights are typical ranges from the fossil record. Every drawing on this page is a stylized diagram made in code.
